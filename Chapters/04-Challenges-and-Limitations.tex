  While Ayang holds promise, its development and deployment are not without challenges. Acknowledging these limitations is crucial for an effective implementation strategy.

  \section{Technical Challenges}
  The primary measure of the AI's success will be its accuracy in two key areas: waste verification and weight estimation.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Image Recognition Accuracy}: The core of Ayang's integrity rests on the AI model's accuracy. Poor lighting, low-resolution images, or occluded trash items could lead to incorrect weight estimations or failed verifications. Our mitigation strategy is twofold: user education (prompting users to take clear photos) and aggressive model fine-tuning.

    \item  \textbf{Malicious Use and "Faking"}: Users could potentially try to cheat the system. For example, a user might dump their own trash just to take a photo of it. We plan to implement several countermeasures:

          \begin{itemize}
            \item Location Verification: Ensuring the "before" and "after" photos are taken at the same location.

            \item Time Stamping: Checking that a reasonable amount of time has passed between the two photos.

            \item AI Anomaly Detection: Training a separate, simpler model to flag suspicious activity patterns (e.g., a user who consistently cleans up the exact same weight of trash).


          \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Scalability and Cost}: As the user base grows, the cost of API calls to the Gemini model and data storage will increase. Optimizing the application by caching results and compressing images will be essential. We will also explore grant opportunities and partnerships to cover operational costs.

  \end{itemize}

  \section{Data and Ethical Challenges}


  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bias in Trash Datasets}: Publicly available datasets for trash may not accurately represent the types of waste found in our specific target communities in Africa. A model trained primarily on Western datasets might be less accurate at identifying local food packaging or other region-specific items. This is why creating our own custom, localized dataset for fine-tuning is a central part of our methodology.

    \item \textbf{Data Privacy}: The app collects user location data. We are committed to ethical data handling. All user data will be anonymized before being used for analysis, and our privacy policy will be transparent about what data is collected and how it is used. We will adhere to responsible data use principles as advocated by the competition.


  \end{itemize}



    \section{Logistical and Community Challenges}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The "Last Mile" Problem}: The app helps with trash collection, but it does not solve the problem of final disposal. If there are no municipal bins or collection points nearby, users may simply be moving trash from one public space to a pile in front of their home. To address this, we plan to use the data generated by the app to partner with local governments or private waste management companies to schedule pickups at designated community collection points.

    \item \textbf{Digital Divide}: While mobile penetration is high, not everyone has a smartphone or affordable data. The app's reach may be limited to more affluent community members. To promote inclusivity, we plan to develop an offline-first functionality and explore community-based models where one person with a smartphone can document the work of a group.

  \end{itemize}

  Addressing these challenges will require a combination of technical refinement, strategic partnerships, and a deep understanding of the communities we aim to serve.
