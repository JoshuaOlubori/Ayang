Sub-Saharan Africa struggles with a waste management crisis, with projections indicating that the region's waste generation will triple by 2050 \citep{Kaza2018}. Improperly managed waste contributes to a host of problems, including environmental pollution, the spread of disease, and the degradation of community spaces. In many municipalities, overburdened or under-resourced formal waste collection systems struggle to keep pace, leaving communities to contend with the consequences.

However, this challenge also presents an opportunity for innovation. The proliferation of mobile technology across the continent, combined with advancements in artificial intelligence, offers new tools for citizen engagement and decentralized problem-solving (rewrite). It is against this context that we developed Ayang, named after the Ibibio word for a sweeping broom.

\vspace{.935em}
Ayang is a web-based platform that reframes waste collection as a rewarding, community-building activity. It leverages the principles of gamification—the application of game-design elements in non-game contexts—to motivate user participation. Research has shown that gamification can be a powerful driver of pro-environmental behavior, increasing engagement and sustaining long-term interest in activities like recycling and energy conservation \citep{Du2020}.

\vspace{.935em}
The technical core of Ayang is its use of Google's Gemini Vision model, a state-of-the-art AI for image analysis. Users take a photo of a littered site, and the model provides an initial assessment of the trash. After the user cleans the area and submits a "clean" photo, the model verifies the cleanup. This AI-driven verification process is crucial for maintaining the integrity of the gamified system and for collecting reliable data. The points awarded to users are directly tied to the estimated weight of the collected trash, creating a direct incentive for more impactful cleanups.

\vspace{.935em}
This project aligns with the goals of the \href{https://deeplearningindaba.com/2025/dli-2025-community-challenge-ai-for-community-impact/}{DLI 2025 Community Challenge} by proposing an AI-driven solution to a real-world African challenge. It is designed to be a practical, community-focused tool that not only improves local environments but also strengthens community bonds and generates valuable data to support broader waste management strategies.
